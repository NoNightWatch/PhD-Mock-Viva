# Fictional output example

[中文](example-output.zh.md) · [Matching input](fictional-paper.md)

This editorially prepared demonstration illustrates fields and questioning routes. It describes no real thesis, person, or viva record and is not a measured model-performance result. Sources refer only to the fictional miniature paper in this repository; no data or page numbers are invented beyond that explicitly fictional input.

## 1. Overall change and a specific factor

**Main question:** The reservation policy and opening hours both changed between the two weeks. How could you determine how much of the change in waiting time came from the reservation policy?

**Thesis evidence:** Section 2 records both changes, non-random assignment, and the absence of a comparison site. Section 3 reports median waiting times. Section 4 acknowledges unresolved attribution.

**Questioning logic:** L09 examines the effect identified by the current design. The author already states the limitation; the follow-up tests whether they can explain it and design a further comparison.

**Follow-up branches:** If the answer is "a decrease of 6 minutes," ask whether that descriptive difference equals the policy's separate effect. If equal opening hours are proposed, ask how changing demand would be handled.

**Answer checkpoints:** Distinguish descriptive change from causation, identify jointly changing conditions, and propose a comparison with remaining limits.

**Boundary:** The available summaries cannot quantify each factor's separate contribution.

## 2. What follows from a genuine improvement in an indicator?

**Main question:** Assuming queue waiting time genuinely decreases, could the overall library experience still become worse? Give an example consistent with that premise.

**Thesis evidence:** Section 1 expects an improved experience. Section 2 does not measure booking effort, accessibility, or satisfaction. Section 4 retains that boundary.

**Questioning logic:** L01 and L07 accept the indicator's improvement and examine its relationship to, and coverage of, overall experience.

**Follow-up branches:** If the answer switches to measurement error, restate that the decrease is accepted. If booking takes longer, ask what additional outcome should be observed.

**Answer checkpoints:** Identify another dimension within the premise, such as booking effort or exclusion of people unable to use the online system, and evidence that could test the explanation.

**Boundary:** These are possible scenarios for investigation; the paper does not establish that they occurred.

## 3. Transfer to another setting

**Main question:** If the method were used at another library, what could be reused directly, and what would need validation?

**Thesis evidence:** Section 2 covers one site, two teaching weeks, and completed visits. Section 3 supports only limited feasibility. Section 5 proposes further comparisons.

**Questioning logic:** L07 and L05 distinguish reusable measurement rules, the scope of generalization, and follow-up design.

**Follow-up branches:** If the same system is considered sufficient, ask how demand, visitors, and opening hours affect interpretation at the new site. If multiple sites are proposed, ask for the comparison unit and primary analysis objective.

**Answer checkpoints:** Define the population and outcomes, include unsuccessful visits, examine site differences and temporal changes, and explain the analysis strategy.

**Boundary:** Multiple sites can strengthen the evidence while still requiring the identification conditions of the chosen comparison.
